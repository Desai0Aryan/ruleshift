# Breakout game research and analysis

Source: earlier ChatGPT session (figures not re-verified; check before citing).

## Case studies
### Flappy Bird (2014)
- Why it spread: trivial controls, brutal difficulty, instant retry, high-score bragging.
- Reported to have earned roughly $50,000/day from ads at peak (contemporary WIRED reporting, per the earlier session).
- Lesson: understandable in seconds, hard to master.

### Wordle (2021-2022)
- Why it spread: one shared daily puzzle for everyone; results shareable without spoiling the answer.
- Grew from tiny numbers to millions of daily players.
- Lesson: give people something to share that pulls friends in.

### Among Us (2020 breakout)
- Why it spread: accusation, lying, and arguing produced funny moments; streamers amplified them.
- Reported 500 million players in November 2020 (Guinness, per earlier session).
- Lesson: gameplay should generate entertaining human reactions.

### Pokemon GO (2016)
- Why it spread: familiar characters + GPS + real-world social activity.
- Reported 500M+ downloads by September 2016.
- Lesson: a game can be a shared experience, not only a screen activity.

### Vampire Survivors (2022)
- Why it spread: simple controls, overwhelming visual feedback, escalating power, satisfying progression.
- Lesson: easy entry plus escalating excitement drives replay.

## Pattern: five ingredients
| Ingredient | Effect |
|---|---|
| Simple hook | Understood immediately |
| Unique mechanic | Something new to experience |
| Replayability | People return unforced |
| Social distribution | Players recruit players |
| Emotional payoff | Excitement, surprise, competition, laughter |

## Key insight
The game should generate its own marketing: Wordle made shareable scores, Among Us made clips, Flappy Bird made high-score challenges. RULESHIFT's equivalent is the mutating puzzle chain and its replay.
